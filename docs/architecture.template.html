Supplier Problem Sheet — how the recommendation pipeline works

An AI assistant that answers supplier problem sheets strictly from the company's own records. It holds no opinion of its own, and when it cannot ground an answer it says so rather than guessing.

Architecture overview · for reviewers and stakeholders · accompanies architecture-runtime.svg and architecture-tooling.svg

The one idea worth taking away. A general AI assistant answers from everything it has ever read. This one is deliberately prevented from doing that. It is shown a small, filtered set of company records and told to restate what those records say — nothing more. Every sentence it produces must be traceable to one of them, and four separate checks run before anything can be sent.

The consequence is a system that refuses more often than a chatbot would, and that is the intended trade. A refusal costs someone ten minutes. A confident, wrong instruction sent to a supplier costs considerably more.

How one problem sheet is answered

Two sources, tried in order

The system is allowed exactly two sources of truth, and it prefers the first.

SourceWhat it isWhy it comes in this order
1 · What we did before Past problem sheets for the same part number, narrowed to those describing a genuinely similar problem. A fix that worked on this part before is the strongest evidence available, and it is already written in the company's own voice.
2 · What the standards require The 0250 engineering standards, searched by section, each passage cited by document and heading. Only consulted when precedent gives nothing. A standard states a requirement; it does not always say what to do about a part that misses it, which makes it the weaker source.

The part number is an exact filter, not a suggestion. A ticket for one part is never answered from another part's history, however similar the wording.

A past fix is sent as it was written

When a past record is judged to be asking the same question — above a confidence bar — its recorded solution is passed through exactly as written. Nothing is reworded and nothing is reviewed, because there is nothing new to review: it is not a new answer, it is one this company has already given.

That places the weight on one judgement: whether the past problem really is the same request. Two suppliers can report an identical defect and ask for opposite things — one to rework and ship, one to ship as-is — and an answer to the first does not answer the second. So the match is made on the request rather than on how alike the two tickets read.

What the reviewer gets instead of a review. Past solutions were written internally and routinely mention attachments, prior phone calls and other suppliers' ticket numbers — useful in the archive, meaningless once forwarded. Anything sent this way is checked for that content and, where it is found, the row carries a note saying so. The note does not stop it; it tells the reviewer which rows to read before passing on.

The four checks, on anything the AI writes

These apply to the second source, where the AI does compose a reply. Every draft passes all four before it can leave. A failure sends it back to be rewritten, up to three attempts, after which the ticket is refused outright. An unverified draft is never sent.

  1. Nothing invented. Every action, measurement, tolerance and specification must appear in the source text. Adding a sensible-sounding safety step is treated as an error, because the system has no way to know whether it is correct here.
  2. Nothing carried over from the old record. Past solutions refer to attachments, prior phone calls and internal ticket numbers. All of those were true of a different problem sheet and are meaningless — or false — on this one.
  3. The right party is being asked to act. Historical solutions are an engineer's own notes, so their instructions are addressed to the customer. Copied across unchanged, they end up telling the supplier to do the customer's job.
  4. It answers what this ticket actually asked. Two suppliers can report the same defect and ask for different outcomes. A reply that would suit both equally well has answered neither.

Checks 2 and 3 exist because real answers failed

Both were added after reviewing live output, not anticipated in design. Two recommendations that had passed every check at the time read:

was sent1. See the feedback in the attachment. 2. Per discussed, rework as attachment shown. 3. After rework, provide photos and related data. 4. ESW#2003… is submitted for these issues.

Nothing there is invented — every line is a faithful quotation from a past record, which is exactly why the original checks passed it. But the supplier receives text and nothing else: there is no attachment, no discussion took place, and that work-request number belongs to somebody else's problem. A later pair told a supplier to issue a waiver — an instrument only the customer can grant, and the very thing that supplier had written in to request.

Both classes are now caught by a fixed rule that runs before the AI reviewer, because an AI asked to catch its own blind spot is what failed in the first place. The fixed rule is also carefully bounded: part numbers, tolerances, durations and the supplier's own legitimate actions must pass it untouched, and rather more of the testing went into that than into the catching.

Why a refusal is a result

"No recommendation available" is recorded as a normal outcome with the reason attached, not as a failure. It means the archive genuinely did not contain an answer to this question — which is worth knowing, and is information the reviewer can act on immediately.

The closest thing that was found is shown alongside it either way, in full, labelled as raw archive text that has not been checked for sending on. A reviewer can then judge the near-miss for themselves rather than being told only that there was one. It is deliberately kept out of the field that gets forwarded: past solutions routinely refer to attachments, prior phone calls and other suppliers' ticket numbers — useful context internally, and false or meaningless the moment they are passed on.

Two situations produce it most often, and they mean quite different things. Either nothing similar enough exists, in which case this is a genuinely novel problem; or something was found but could not be turned into anything the supplier could act on, which usually says more about how the original record was written than about the defect.

A person reviews everything

No recommendation reaches a supplier unread, and this is a permanent feature of the design rather than a caution to be relaxed once confidence grows. The system's job is to find the precedent, draft the reply and show its sources; deciding whether to send it stays with the reviewer.

The supporting tools

The tool that matters most day to day is the bulk test: a sheet of tickets goes in, and the same sheet comes back with the recommendation, the reason and the source records set beside each original row. It is how quality gets judged across a batch rather than one ticket at a time, and the source column is usually what explains a surprising answer.

What it cannot do

LimitationWhat it means in practice
The confidence gates are not yet calibrated The thresholds deciding "similar enough" are provisional and have not been measured against the live service. They may currently be too strict or too loose. The batch evaluator exists to settle them, and that should happen before anyone reads much into a pass rate.
Answer quality is bounded by record quality Where past solutions are process boilerplate — "rework as discussed", "see the attachment" — there is no technical content to restate, and the system correctly refuses. The pass rate therefore measures the archive at least as much as it measures the AI.
It does not make dispositions Asked "may we ship this as-is?", it can only report what was done in similar cases before. It will not make the accept, rework or scrap decision, and where precedent does not answer that question it refuses rather than implying one.
Near-identical tickets can attract the same answer Matching is on the whole problem description. When two tickets share a long background passage and differ in one sentence, that sentence can be outweighed. Check 4 turns the resulting non-answer into a refusal, but does not yet produce the right answer.
